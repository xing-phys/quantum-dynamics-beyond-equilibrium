# Teaching architecture and source policy

Literature cutoff: 20 October 2026. Primary papers are verified through their
arXiv full text and publisher records, with topic audit notes in this directory.

The central question is what survives the averaging that produces a density
matrix. Four foundation chapters teach the mathematics used in the research.
Three develop projected ensembles and constrained universality. Three
distinguish time sampling, aperiodic drive complexity, and resource transitions.
Four develop dynamical maps, multi-time memory, constrained Hilbert spaces, and
driven phases. A computational laboratory and research directions connect the
concepts. Appendices supply mathematical tools, selected worked exercise
solutions, and an annotated primary-source guide.

Each chapter introduces unfamiliar terms, specifies assumptions, provides
derivations and small examples, and separates exact identities, source theorems,
numerical evidence, and conjectures.

Figures are original illustrations or generated by included Julia scripts.
No publication figure is copied. Finite-size computations are illustrations,
not evidence for a new phase diagram.

Notation: hbar=1; d_A and d_B are Hilbert-space dimensions; natural logarithms
unless stated; moment order k; M_k is a Born-weighted state moment; trace
distance includes 1/2. Each chapter defines specialized notation.

The exposition is independently written and AI-assisted. It is not affiliated
with or endorsed by the authors of the cited research.
